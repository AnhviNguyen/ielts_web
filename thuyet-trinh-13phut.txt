SLIDE 2 — CONTEXT + PROBLEM · 1 minute 30 seconds
================================================================================

[Point to each stat on the slide as you speak]

Context: in Vietnam, an estimated 300,000 IELTS tests are taken each year —
source Vietnam.vn and the Vietnam Institute of Educational Sciences.

According to the IELTS partners global report 2023–2024, Vietnam’s average
Academic band is 6.2, ranking 29th out of 40 countries. About 70% of candidates
score between 5.5 and 7.0; the most common band is 6.0 at 21%.

Notably, Vietnam’s average Speaking score is only 5.7 — below the global average
of 6.3.

In parallel, the 2025 national high school English exam averaged 5.38 out of 10;
only 141 students nationwide scored a perfect 10 — that is 0.04% — per MOET via
Vietnam.vn. Many graduates therefore shift to IELTS: 27% of Vietnamese candidates
reach band 7.0 or higher on Academic IELTS, according to the British Council, and
over 61 universities accept IELTS score conversion for admission in 2025 — VTC
News.

[Pivot to the problem]

Yet self-study remains fragmented. Many learners over-focus on Reading and neglect
Writing and Speaking. Writing and Speaking feedback through tutors is slow and
expensive. There is no personal band forecast and no five-day study plan tied to
weak skills. That is the gap LinguaIELTS targets.

================================================================================
SLIDE 3 — MARKET + OUR APPROACH · 1 minute 30 seconds
================================================================================

We compare against real IELTS platforms — not generic English apps.

British Council and IDP: trusted content and mocks, but no personal ML forecast
or five-day AI plan. IELTS Online Tests is strong for Reading and Listening, but
lacks unified four-skill AI and forecasting. Migii is a popular Vietnamese IELTS
app with partial planning, but no hybrid Speaking pipeline or time-series analytics.
TestGlider has strong AI Writing and Speaking, but it is closed SaaS — no dual
forecast layer or open deployable stack.

LinguaIELTS fills four gaps:

  • “Where am I heading?” — NeuralProphet 14-day per-skill trends plus RandomForest
    next-week status.
  • “What should I do today?” — a five-day AI plan from history, progress, and
    target band.
  • Slow, costly Speaking — Whisper, Wav2Vec2, and LLM pipeline returning results
    in minutes.
  • Siloed tools — one score_history and one dashboard.

================================================================================
SLIDE 4 — SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE · 1 minute
================================================================================

The deployed architecture is simple:

The learner uses a browser and accesses the Vue web app — dashboard, practice, and
forecast tabs. The frontend calls the FastAPI server over HTTPS for login, scoring,
AI, and study plans.

On the server side, three storage groups:
  • PostgreSQL — users, scores, plans;
  • Media — quiz audio and images;
  • ML weights on the same server — Whisper, Wav2Vec2, RandomForest joblib.

The full stack is running in production — not localhost-only.

================================================================================
SLIDE 5 — FORECAST · 1 minute 30 seconds
================================================================================

Our first technical contribution: personal band forecasting — two complementary
models, not one fused model.

NeuralProphet answers: where is this skill going over the next 14 days? It uses
daily data, needs at least 14 days of practice, trains per user per skill, and
shows charts, MAE, and alerts.

RandomForest answers a shorter question: next week, does overall performance go
up, stay flat, or decline? It uses weekly buckets, needs two weeks of data, and
runs as one offline global model — macro MAE about 0.22 band on synthetic data,
roughly 13% better than a naive baseline at 0.25.

Purpose is formative only — prioritize weak skills and warn early stagnation —
not replace official IDP or British Council exam scores.

================================================================================
SLIDE 6 — FORECAST FLOW · 45 seconds
================================================================================

Actual flow: the user logs in, completes practice, and the server scores and saves
daily bands into score_history.

Fewer than 14 days — the Forecast tab shows cold start. At 14 days or more — we
train NeuralProphet, predict, and show the 14-day chart plus next-week trend on
the Forecast page.

All scores come from logged-in submissions scored on the server — users do not
self-report bands.

================================================================================
SLIDE 7 — SPEAKING · 1 minute 30 seconds
================================================================================

Our second contribution: hybrid IELTS Speaking scoring.

Four steps: Whisper transcribes audio; Wav2Vec2 fine-tuned on SpeechOcean762 scores
pronunciation — PCC approximately 0.693 on validation; an LLM scores Fluency,
Lexical Resource, Grammar, and checks on-topic response; the server aggregates
the IELTS band.

Important metric distinction:

  • PCC 0.693 measures correlation of 0–10 pronunciation sub-scores with experts
    on a read-aloud corpus — measured in our notebook.
  • GOPT SOTA on the same corpus reaches utterance PCC 0.742 — Gong et al., ICASP
    2022 — an academic benchmark, not our product claim.
  • ±0.5 or ±1.0 IELTS Speaking band versus a real examiner — we have NOT measured
    examiner MAE yet; we state this openly in the final slide.

App quota: 20 AI Speaking evaluations per user per day — configured in limits.py.

================================================================================
SLIDE 8 — SPEAKING FLOW · 45 seconds
================================================================================

The user records audio and uploads while logged in. The server checks quota, runs
Whisper and Wav2Vec2 for the acoustic path, the LLM for content rubric scoring,
merges the band, saves History, and returns the UI — within minutes.

Q&A — LIKELY QUESTIONS & PERSUASIVE ANSWERS
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. TOPIC & SCOPE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q1. Does this app replace high school exam prep?
→ No. LinguaIELTS trains four IELTS skills. Many graduates choose IELTS for
  university admission and study abroad — the app supports that path, not the
  national graduation exam itself.

Q2. Are AI scores official IELTS bands?
→ No. Formative practice only. Not an IDP/British Council certificate. The UI
  includes disclaimers. AI bands show what to improve during practice.

Q3. Why so much ML in a CS graduation project?
→ This is an AI-integrated EdTech product. Dual forecast (NeuralProphet + RF) and
  hybrid Speaking (Wav2Vec2 + LLM) are technical contributions with cited papers
  and offline metrics — beyond a simple CRUD app.

Q4. Deployed or localhost only?
→ Deployed on a production server: nginx, Vue, FastAPI, PostgreSQL. We can demo
  Forecast, Speaking, and Study Plan live.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. FORECAST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q5. What are the requirements for forecasting?
→ Logged-in practice on the platform with scores saved to score_history.
  NeuralProphet: ≥14 days. RandomForest next-week: ≥2 weeks. Otherwise cold start
  with a clear UI message.

Q6. Why two models instead of one?
→ Different questions and horizons: NP = 14-day per-skill time series; RF = one-week
  overall trend (up/flat/down). Fusion needs examiner labels we do not yet have.

Q7. What data trains RandomForest? Can we trust it?
→ Currently offline synthetic data in ielts_model/ — macro MAE 0.22 vs naive 0.25.
  Honest answer: not yet validated on a real user cohort. Mitigation: retrain on
  production score_history plus a 30–50 user pilot.

Q8. What is NeuralProphet MAE?
→ It varies per user — shown on the UI after a 3–7 day holdout. No single global
  number because models are per-user, per-skill.

Q9. Does forecast match real exam scores?
→ We do not claim that. Forecast reflects practice trajectory on our platform.
  Real exam scores depend on test form, stress, and examiner factors — outside MVP
  scope.

Q10. How do you know scores belong to that user?
→ JWT authentication; every submission tied to user_id; server-side scoring;
  score_history keyed by (user, skill, date). Direct band self-entry API is disabled.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
C. SPEAKING & WRITING AI
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q11. How is Speaking scored?
→ Whisper → transcript; Wav2Vec2 → pronunciation (PCC ≈ 0.693 on SpeechOcean762);
  LLM → FC, LR, GRA + on-topic check; server merges IELTS band. Minutes, not days.

Q12. What does PCC 0.693 mean? At ±0.5 band, how accurate is it?
→ PCC measures correlation of 0–10 pronunciation sub-scores with experts on a
  read-aloud corpus — NOT the percentage of full IELTS Speaking bands matching an
  examiner within ±0.5. We have not measured examiner band agreement yet. On the
  pronunciation leg only, PCC ≈ 0.7 typically implies roughly 65–75% agreement
  within ±0.5 on a 0–10 pronunciation scale (order-of-magnitude, not IELTS band).
  ±1.0 IELTS band vs examiner: not measured — formative drift of 0.5–1.0 band may
  occur without calibration.

Q13. What is GOPT 0.742 and why mention it?
→ SOTA benchmark on the same SpeechOcean762 corpus (Gong et al., ICASP 2022). Our
  Wav2Vec2 reaches 0.693 — about 0.05 lower — setting realistic expectations, not
  claiming SOTA.

Q14. Why not use LLM only for Speaking?
→ LLMs cannot hear pronunciation. Wav2Vec2 handles the acoustic signal; the LLM
  handles content and task response — hybrid design is intentional.

Q15. Is Writing quota-limited?
→ AI Writing submission is unlimited per day in code. It still consumes OpenRouter
  tokens. Writing coach chat: 40 messages per day.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
D. OPENROUTER, RAG, OPERATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q16. What is OpenRouter?
→ An API gateway to LLMs (Gemini, Claude, etc.) billed per token. Implemented in
  openrouter_client.py — free → paid cascade, key rotation on 402/429.

Q17. What happens when quota runs out?
→ Two layers: (A) server OpenRouter credit exhausted → entire system cannot grade AI;
  (B) per-user app quotas — Speaking 20/day, chat 40/day, tutor 120/month (limits.py).
  Users may supply their own OpenRouter key in profile settings.

Q18. Why no RAG from the start?
→ Thesis scope and licensed PDF copyright. MVP uses prompts plus DB content. Phase 3:
  licensed RAG → fewer tokens, syllabus-grounded feedback, less hallucination.

Q19. LLM operating cost?
→ Depends on users and tokens per request. Mitigation: free model cascade, user keys,
  RAG-shortened prompts, premium/B2B pricing — outlined in Chapter 4 BMC.

Q20. Redis/Celery?
→ Demo stack: four services — gateway, frontend, api, db — ML inline in API. Celery
  is optional when scaling. Pilot target: under 50 concurrent users — stated in SWOT.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
E. COMPETITORS & CONTRIBUTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Q21. How are you different from TestGlider / IOT / Migii?
→ IOT: strong R/L mocks, no full forecast + AI plan stack. TestGlider: strong AI
  W/S but closed SaaS. Migii: Vietnamese IELTS app without hybrid acoustic + dual
  forecast + open deploy. We integrate four skills, dual forecast, five-day plan,
  and deployable architecture.

Q22. What are the thesis contributions?
→ (1) Dual-layer learning analytics on score_history; (2) Hybrid Speaking with
  published PCC; (3) Deployable MVP + UML; (4) Methods grounded in cited literature:
  Triebe 2021, Breiman 2001, Baevski 2020, Zhang 2021, Gong 2022 GOPT.

Q23. Biggest weakness?
→ AI bands not examiner-calibrated and no large user study — stated openly with
  concrete Phase 2 mitigations.

Q24. Post-graduation roadmap?
→ Phase 2: examiner corpus, MAE reporting, 30–50 user pilot, RF retrain on real data.
  Phase 3: RAG, mobile, freemium payment, B2B center licensing.
